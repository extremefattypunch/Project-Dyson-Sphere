# M3: Ground sites, Earth rotation, retarded-time targeting, occultation and access

Status: implemented; all gates pass, with every SPICE test run (none skipped). Not committed (a
separate step commits).

## A. Summary

- **Ground sites** (`helioswarm.ground`).
  - WGS 84 geodetic → Earth-fixed Cartesian conversion, and the local east/north/up basis
    (up = ellipsoid normal).
  - `GroundSite` holds only Earth-fixed (`IAU_EARTH`) coordinates.
  - `SiteTrajectory` holds the inertial J2000/SSB states together with the local basis in
    J2000. The two are never mixed (PDF §3).
- **Earth orientation.** It is injectable, like the ephemeris provider:
  - `SpiceEarthOrientation` uses SPICE `sxform("IAU_EARTH", "J2000", et)` from
    `pck00011.tpc`, giving the rotation and its exact derivative;
  - `UniformEarthRotation` is a kernel-free test double;
  - the site state is `r = r_⊕ + R r_ef` and `v = v_⊕ + Ṙ r_ef`.
- **IAU_EARTH is a documented approximation.** Against astropy's IERS-based ITRS → GCRS
  transformation, run offline with the bundled IERS-B data, an equatorial site differs by:
  - 6.14 km in 2005, 7.89 km in 2020 and 8.65 km in 2023;
  - 3.7–4.4e-5 rad in pole direction (nutation omitted);
  - about 1.2e-3 rad in rotation angle in 2020. *Corrected in the review (see "Review
    outcomes"):* this is the IAU W definition against the IERS Earth Rotation Angle, not
    nutation or UT1 alone: a constant −8.2e-4 rad (−11 s) offset at J2000 (W₀ matches ERA only
    for TT − UT1 = 75.06 s) plus a −4.04e-5 rad/yr drift at constant TT − UT1. At the simulated
    epochs it is −1.64e-3 rad (~23 s, 10.5 km) in 2030 and −2.04e-3 rad (28 s, 13.0 km) in
    2040.

  The consequence is documented in `docs/model_assumptions.md` (M3): the site is shifted along
  its parallel, which moves the diurnal access timing by about 23 s in 2030 (growing ~0.55 s
  per year). This is not a simulated pointing error.
- **Retarded-time targeting** (`helioswarm.visibility.solve_light_time`, PDF §7.1).
  - Fixed-point iteration, vectorised over the grid, with a tolerance and an iteration cap
    from the configuration.
  - A bracketed `brentq` fallback, flagged per epoch.
  - Stored per epoch: `t_emit_tdb_s`, `t_arrival_tdb_s`, iteration count, method, residual and
    a convergence flag.
  - `models.light_time = false` gives the named instantaneous approximation.
- **Access** (`compute_access` → `AccessResult`, the PDF p.4 object, extended).
  - Range, range rate, light time and dτ/dt.
  - Beam direction: the geometric retarded direction, plus the **transmitter-aberration-corrected
    direction**, which is implemented rather than only estimated. Its size is 5.9e-5 rad maximum
    for the MVP year.
  - The target-motion point-ahead and the analytic inertial pointing rate.
  - Apparent elevation and azimuth, and the Sun separation, all in the receiver's rest frame.
  - Earth blockage: exact segment–ellipsoid test in `IAU_EARTH`, with a standoff.
  - Sun blockage: limb plus angular margin. Moon blockage: lunar radius. Each occulter is tested
    in its own comoving frame.
  - Flags: `below_min_elevation`, `sun_avoidance_violation`, `visible`, `occulted_by`.
- **Satellite illumination** (`satellite_illumination` → `IlluminationResult`).
  - Conical shadow with penumbra, from the apparent-disk overlap area of the Sun and the
    Earth/Moon.
  - Light-time-retarded body positions as seen from the satellite.
  - Occulter flag and a flag for several simultaneous occulters.
- **PDF §7.3 diagnostics** (`summarize_access` → `AccessSummary`).
  - Fractions: access, occultation (Earth/Sun/Moon), below minimum elevation, Sun avoidance.
  - Range and light-time min/mean/max, range-rate extremes, pointing-rate mean/max, aberration
    and point-ahead maxima, solver diagnostics.
  - Orbit drift against the new L0 reference, `propagate.l0_reference_trajectory`.
- **Exit criteria met.**
  - The synthetic-geometry and Earth-rotation tests pass.
  - The MVP-config year runs with SPICE: 8767 hourly epochs, access computed in about 1.2–1.6 s.
    The PDF §7.3 diagnostics come from the real de440s/pck00011 geometry (numbers in D).

## E. Assumptions and equations implemented

All equations, validity ranges, error estimates and references are in
`docs/model_assumptions.md`, section "M3". In short:

- **WGS 84:** `N = a/√(1 − e² sin²φ)`; `x, y = (N + h) cos φ (cos λ, sin λ)`,
  `z = (N(1 − e²) + h) sin φ`; up = `(cos φ cos λ, cos φ sin λ, sin φ)` (NGA.STND.0036).
- **Earth orientation (IAU_EARTH, pck00011, IAU WGCCRE 2009):**
  `R = ([W]₃[π/2 − δ₀]₁[π/2 + α₀]₃)ᵀ`, with `W = 190.147° + 360.9856235° d` (TDB days). Its
  accuracy is measured against IERS (above).
- **Site state:** `r = r_⊕ + R r_ef`, `v = v_⊕ + Ṙ r_ef`.
- **Light time (PDF §7.1):** `cτ = |r_site(t_e + τ) − r_sat(t_e)|`, solved by fixed point with
  a brentq fallback. It is Newtonian: the Shapiro delay (1.3e-5 s for the MVP geometry) and
  light bending (~2.4 km transverse at the receiver) are neglected and stated.
- **Beam direction:** `u = (r_site(t_a) − r_sat(t_e))/|…|`.
- **Transmitter pointing:** `u′ = [u + (γ−1)(u·b̂)b̂ − γβ]/[γ(1 − β·u)]` (Lorentz aberration,
  Rindler 2006 §4.4).
- **Pointing rate:** `|(I − uuᵀ)(v_site(1+τ′) − v_sat)|/|ρ|`, with
  `τ′ = u·(v_site − v_sat)/(c − u·v_site)`.
- **Receiver directions:** apparent, in the receiver frame (aberrated with `v_site`), without
  refraction. Elevation is `atan2(s·up, |s_horizontal|)` and azimuth is
  `atan2(s·east, s·north)`.
- **Occultation:** per-body comoving segment `[r_sat(t_e) − r_B(t_e), r_site(t_a) − r_B(t_a)]`,
  exact for uniform motion (error ≤ |a_B|τ²/8).
  - Earth: scaled-sphere segment test in `IAU_EARTH` with a 1 m standoff.
  - Sun: `R_block = d sin(asin(R☉/d) + margin)`.
  - Moon: `R_moon`.
- **Illumination:** hidden fraction = circle-overlap(asin(R☉/d☉), asin(R_B/d_B), θ)/(π r☉²),
  using retarded body positions (Montenbruck & Gill 2000, §3.4.2).
- **Diagnostics (PDF §7.3):** grid-sample fractions on the TDB-uniform grid, and
  `|r_L1 − r_L0|` with `r_L0` from `l0_reference_trajectory`.

## F. Known limitations and next milestone

### Deviations from the brief, and the choices behind them

1. **The MVP geometry is not "30° ahead of the Earth".** The brief expected a range of
   ~0.52 au. The MVP configuration sets `true_anomaly_deg: 30` with RAAN = ω = i = 0, which is
   ecliptic longitude 30°. On 2030-01-01 the Earth is at heliocentric longitude ~100°, so the
   satellite *trails* the Earth by ~70°, and the range is 1.12–1.18 au (light time ~575 s).
   The tests check the actual configuration (the 70° separation is asserted from the raw
   ephemeris). The configuration was not changed: that is a scenario decision, not an M3 one.
2. **The access fraction is not equal to the diurnal elevation fraction at hourly steps.** The
   satellite's synodic day is within minutes of the solar day, so the hourly grid samples
   nearly the same 24 hour angles every day. The hourly access fraction 0.4220 differs from the
   analytic 0.4420 by 0.020 (≤ 1/24, asserted). A 5-minute, 30-day run agrees to < 0.005
   (asserted). This is a real sampling caveat for M6 availability metrics, recorded in
   `model_assumptions.md`.
3. **Solar-limb margin.** The brief suggested `R☉(1 + margin)`. The schema already had the
   angular field `models.sun_occultation_limb_margin_deg` ("seen from the receiver"), so it was
   kept and implemented exactly: `R_block = d sin(asin(R☉/d) + margin)`. With margin 0 this is
   the physical solar sphere.
4. **Sun position for the Sun test.** The brief suggested "the Sun at the epoch the beam passes
   closest (t_emit + fraction)". Instead each occulter is tested in its own comoving frame
   using its positions at t_e and t_a. This is exact for uniform motion; the error bound
   |a|τ²/8 is about 0.03 m for the Sun and ≤ 0.4 km for the Moon, and a brute-force test
   confirms it. The same treatment is used for the Moon and the Earth.
5. **Receiver directions are apparent (aberrated), not geometric.** This is a change made
   during M3 after the mutation check. The barycentric direction `−u` misplaces the horizon by
   up to |v⊕|/c ≈ 1e-4 rad relative to the Earth-frame photon path used by the blockage test.
   Elevation, azimuth and Sun separation therefore use the receiver-frame direction. The two
   tests now agree to the rotation speed/c = 1.6e-6 rad, which is asserted over the MVP year.
6. **Transmitter aberration is implemented, not only estimated.** It is stored as
   `transmit_direction_tx_frame_unit` and `transmitter_aberration_rad`, alongside the geometric
   `transmit_direction_unit`.
7. **Configuration additions.** Three numerical fields were added (light-time tolerance,
   iteration cap, Earth standoff). The hash was re-pinned with the history explained in the
   test; `schema_version` is unchanged.
8. **Published station.** *Superseded in the review:* the NGS datasheet of station KG0640
   (MEADES RANCH RESET, NAD 83(2011) on GRS 80) is now used; published geodetic → published
   X, Y, Z agree to ≤ 0.46 mm. The conversion is also checked against two independent
   implementations (SPICE `georec`, astropy/ERFA `gd2gc`) to < 1 mm, plus exact closed-form
   points.
9. **L1 step in the year test.** The integration tests propagate with a 1-day step cap instead
   of the configured 300 s. M2 measured the two to agree to 4.6 cm over the year, and the cap
   keeps the test module at ~10 s instead of ~60 s.

### Limitations

- **IAU_EARTH accuracy.** About 8 km at the surface in 2020 and ~10.5 km in 2030 (growing
  ~0.26 km/yr at constant TT − UT1); see E, "Review outcomes" and `model_assumptions.md`. An
  IERS/ITRS Earth orientation is needed only for comparisons with real past passes. Future
  UT1 is unpredictable anyway.
- **Not modelled:**
  - atmospheric refraction, where the min-elevation constraint applies to the unrefracted
    apparent elevation;
  - relativistic light propagation (Shapiro delay, bending; sizes stated);
  - polar motion and nutation (inside the IAU_EARTH approximation);
  - terrain;
  - limb darkening in the illumination fraction;
  - planets as occulters.
- **Two occulters at once.** Their fractions are summed, an upper bound on the loss, and the
  epoch is flagged.
- **Disabled checks.** Occultation checks disabled in the configuration are not evaluated
  (flag false, distance NaN). The Moon must be available from the provider when
  `moon_occultation` is on.
- **Pointing quantities are geometric.** They contain no pointing-error model; pointing bias
  and jitter belong to M5.
- **Storage hygiene.** One probe run with `python -I` ignored `PYTHONPYCACHEPREFIX` and wrote
  `src/helioswarm/__pycache__` (2 files) inside the repository. It was deleted, and the
  repository has no `__pycache__`, `.venv` or tool caches.

**A. IAU_EARTH rotation-angle error (findings 1, 7, 10).** Verified with a scratch probe:
`pxform('IAU_EARTH','J2000')` against ERFA `c2t06a` (UT1 = UTC, no polar motion) gives
−7.9e-4 rad (2000), −1.26e-3 (2020), −1.64e-3 (2030), −2.04e-3 (2040), as the reviewers
reported. The offset equals, to ≤ 2.4e-8 rad, the closed form
`(280.147° − 0.641° T + 360.9856235° d) − ERA(UT1)` (IERS 2010 eq. 5.15). Nutation therefore
plays no part in it, and neither does the equation of the equinoxes. It has two parts:

- a constant −0.047° (W₀ corresponds to TT − UT1 = 75.06 s; the actual value is 63.8 s);
- a rate deficit of −6.3e-6 deg/day (−4.04e-5 rad/yr, −0.55 s of rotation per year at
  constant TT − UT1), partly offset in the past by the growth of TT − UT1.

Changes:

- The `ground.py` module docstring, the astropy-test docstring (its tolerances are now
  stated as empirical) and `model_assumptions.md` (M3, Earth orientation) were rewritten.
- A new ERFA test, `test_iau_earth_rotation_angle_offset_is_the_w_definition_against_era`,
  pins the closed form, the J2000 constant, the 2030/2040 values (10.5/13.0 km) and the drift.
- The 2030 consequence is now ~23 s, 10.5 km. The astropy test's 13 km bound is stated to
  hold only for the past epochs it tests.

One detail of finding 10 does not hold. Its claim that "only the growth since 2000 matches the
change in ΔT" mixes up the two parts: since 2000, ΔT growth (+5.5 s) and the rate deficit
(−11 s over 20 yr) partly cancel. This does not change the fix.

**B. Sites below the ellipsoid (finding 2).** Reproduced: at h = −300 m and −1000 m, rays at
+1e-5 … +1e-3 rad of elevation were flagged as Earth-blocked. The ellipsoid with axes
(a + h, b + h) is not the parallel surface, and the site lay 0.42 mm (−300 m) and 1.41 mm
(−1000 m) inside it.

- The fix tests sites with h < 0 against the WGS 84 ellipsoid scaled by
  `k = √((x² + y²)/a² + z²/b²)`. This similar ellipsoid passes exactly through the site
  (`_surface_scale_through`). Its normal differs from the geodetic up by 5.3e-7 rad at
  h = −1000 m.
- New test `test_sites_below_the_ellipsoid_are_consistent_near_the_horizon`
  (h = −300/−1000 m; e = −1e-5 blocked, +1e-5 and +1e-4 clear).
- The config description of `earth_occultation_standoff_m` and `model_assumptions.md` were
  updated. The description is not part of the config hash, which is unchanged.

**C. Sun separation with a moving receiver (finding 3).** Confirmed: both existing tests used
a static receiver. New test `test_sun_separation_with_a_moving_receiver_and_a_moving_sun`:

- Earth moves at 3e4 m/s ⊥ to the Sun line and the Sun moves at 2e4 m/s.
- The expected value comes from the closed-form light time, the closed-form retarded Sun epoch
  and first-order aberration of both directions. Tolerance 4β² = 4e-8 rad; measured 2.5e-9 rad.
- The test also asserts that the result differs from the geometric angle (2.3e-5 rad), from an
  unretarded Sun (6.7e-5 rad) and from an unaberrated Sun direction (1.0e-4 rad).

**D. Summary aggregation (finding 4).** New test
`test_summary_aggregates_every_occulter_and_averages_light_time`. It hand-sets the flags on a
real result (`dataclasses.replace` re-validates the result) and asserts:

- the union 4/8 (the sum would be 5/8);
- a Moon-only blockage counts toward the occultation fraction;
- the mean light time is 2.0 (the median is 1.0);
- `light_time_mean_s = mean(range)/c` on the unmodified result.

`multiple_occulters` is now asserted False in the full-sun and umbra cases.

**E. SPICE end-to-end check (finding 5).** New test
`test_retarded_targeting_and_point_ahead_match_spice_spkcpt` (`spice`). It uses the Mars
barycentre as the transmitter, a site at 35° N 110° W 1500 m and 240 epochs in 2030, and
compares with `spkcpt` `XCN`/`XCN+S`:

| Quantity | Measured | Tolerance |
|---|---|---|
| Light time | 4.5e-13 s | 1e-9 s |
| Geometric direction | 2.0e-16 rad | 1e-12 rad |
| Aberration-corrected direction | 1.4e-9 rad | 1e-8 rad (O(β²); SPICE is first order) |
| Aberration (size) | 8.1–8.4e-5 rad | n/a |

**F. Multi-site configuration (finding 6).** New test
`test_access_from_config_handles_every_site_and_checks_the_kernel_set`:

- Two sites in config order give one `AccessResult` each, with matching names, and each is
  identical to `compute_access` for that site alone.
- The two sites differ by > 0.5 rad in elevation, so reusing the first site would fail.
- A kernel handle with another `kernel_set_id` raises `KernelSetMismatchError`.
  `frames.ephemeris_kernel_set` is a schema literal, so the mismatch is produced through the
  handle, as in M2.

**G. Published station (findings 8, 12).** New test `test_published_station_meades_ranch_reset`.

- Source: NGS datasheet KG0640 (MEADES RANCH RESET), retrieved 2026-11-12. It gives the
  NAD 83(2011) geodetic position and X, Y, Z on GRS 80.
- Our conversion with GRS 80 and with WGS 84 agrees with the published X, Y, Z to ≤ 0.46 mm.
- Tolerance 1.5 mm per component, from the rounding of the published values plus the 0.11 mm
  ellipsoid difference.
- Recorded in `data_provenance.md` and `validation_report.md` (§13.1).

**H. `visible` responds to every occultation flag (finding 9, major).** Confirmed: the Sun-limb,
superior-conjunction and Moon-edge tests asserted only the flags, and the Earth-behind case
was also below the minimum elevation. Changes:

- The Sun-limb (both margins), superior-conjunction and Moon-edge tests now assert
  `visible == not blocked`, with `below_min_elevation` and `sun_avoidance_violation` asserted
  False, so that only the occultation can decide.
- The geodetic-horizon Earth test repeats each case with `min_elevation_rad = −0.1`: an
  Earth-blocked beam above the minimum elevation is not visible, and a clear one is.
- The summary test above covers a Moon-only occultation fraction.

Mutation check (scratch script, source restored afterwards). Each of these was applied alone
and is caught by the new tests:

- `visible` without the Earth flag;
- `visible` without the Sun flag;
- `visible` without the Moon flag;
- `blocked` without the Moon;
- `blocked` with the Earth only;
- median light time;
- `multiple_occulters` with ≥ 1;
- Sun separation without the receiver aberration of the Sun;
- Sun separation with an unretarded Sun;
- no surface scaling below the ellipsoid.

**I. Diurnal aliasing of access fractions (finding 11).** Confirmed: 0.4220 hourly against
the analytic 0.4420. The code now reports the sampling quality rather than passing the bias on
silently:

- `AccessSummary.hour_angle_max_gap_rad` is the largest gap between the sampled satellite hour
  angles at the site. It comes from the new public `satellite_hour_angle_rad` and
  `max_circular_gap_rad`.
- The `AccessSummary` docstring explains the aliasing and the rough size of the error,
  gap/(2π) per window edge.
- MVP year, hourly: gap 8.6° (gap/2π = 0.024, against the observed 0.020); asserted in
  (5°, 15°), with |error| ≤ 2·gap/2π.
- 30 days at 5 min: gap 0.07° (asserted < 0.2°).
- New unit test `test_hour_angle_and_sampling_gap`: H closed form for a uniformly rotating
  Earth, gap 2π/360, the hourly-aliasing gap 2π/24, and 2π for a non-rotating Earth.
- The MVP output step is left unchanged: that is a scenario decision for M6, which must check
  this diagnostic.

**J. IlluminationResult provenance (finding 13).** Added `source_ids` (the ephemeris kernel
ids, required non-empty) and `time_scale = "TDB"`, both validated in `__post_init__`, plus
per-row length checks. Tested in the full-sun case.
