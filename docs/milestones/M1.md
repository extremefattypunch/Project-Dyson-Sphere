# M1: Analytic two-body orbit (L0): constants, frames, elements ↔ state, Kepler propagation

Status: implemented; all gates pass. Not committed (a separate step commits).

## A. Summary

- **`helioswarm.constants`** is now the single home of every fixed physical constant, each with
  its source:
  - au and c (both exact);
  - GM☉ = 1.3271244004127942e20 m³ s⁻² (DE440, from NAIF `gm_de440.tpc`, which was read and
    hashed, not recalled);
  - σ (CODATA 2018);
  - nominal S☉ = 1361 W m⁻² and R☉ (IAU 2015 B3);
  - WGS 84 a and 1/f;
  - the IERS-2010 ERA rate and the stellar ("sidereal") day derived from it;
  - the J2000 obliquity 84381.448″ and arcsec→rad.

  The config default `constants.solar_constant_1au_W_m2` now references the module constant,
  so the number appears once. The value is unchanged, and so is the config hash.
- **`helioswarm.frames`** has these parts:
  - **`CartesianState` / `CartesianTrajectory`** are frozen, with read-only, validated arrays.
    They carry `frame`, `origin`, `epoch_tdb_s` and `time_scale="TDB"`.
  - **Rotations.** The fixed ECLIPJ2000 → J2000 rotation `R_x(−ε₀)` is a pure function, plus
    the inverse, vector, state and trajectory transforms.
  - **`require_same_frame_and_origin`** is the guard against combining vectors in different
    frames or origins.
- **`helioswarm.orbit`** (L0) has these parts:
  - **`OrbitalElements`**: a frozen dataclass, validated for elliptic orbits.
  - **Conversions.** `elements_to_state` and `state_to_elements`, with documented singular
    conventions (circular, equatorial, both, retrograde).
  - **Anomalies.** Conversions that are continuous across revolutions.
  - **Kepler solver.** A vectorised bracketed-Newton solver with a convergence diagnostic,
    which raises `KeplerConvergenceError` on failure.
  - **`propagate_kepler`.** Forward and backward propagation over an array of epochs, taking
    elements or a state. It returns a trajectory plus solver diagnostics.
  - **Invariants.** Energy, angular-momentum vector, eccentricity vector, period and mean
    motion.
  - **Config hook.** `elements_from_config`.
- **Tests.** 164 new discriminating tests. Each M1 acceptance criterion has at least one test
  against an independent reference, and 16 deliberate source mutations are all caught (§D).

## E. Assumptions and equations implemented

All equations, conventions, validity ranges and references are listed in
`docs/model_assumptions.md`, section "M1". In short:

- Two-body problem of a massless particle about a point-mass Sun, μ = GM☉ (DE440,
  TDB-compatible). Elliptic orbits only.
- Kepler III `T = 2π√(a³/μ)`; orbit equation `r = p/(1+e cos ν)`; perifocal velocity
  `√(μ/p)(−sin ν, e + cos ν)`; vis-viva energy `−μ/2a`; `h = r × v`;
  `e = (v × h)/μ − r/r`. Sources: Vallado 4th ed. ch. 2 and Alg. 9/10 (RV2COE/COE2RV);
  Curtis.
- Anomalies in the Broucke & Cefola (1973) continuous form. Kepler's equation is solved by
  bracketed Newton with Danby's (1987) starter, clipped to [x, min(x+e, π)]. Convergence in
  exact arithmetic follows from monotonicity and convexity on [0, π]; in float64 the stop is
  |ΔE| ≤ 1e-12 rad or a round-off-level residual |f| ≤ 4 ε max(x, E) (added in review).
- Singular conventions: equatorial (sin i < 1e-13) gives Ω = 0 and i ∈ {0, π}; circular
  (e < 1e-13) gives e = 0, ω = 0 and ν = u; both gives ν = true longitude.
- ECLIPJ2000 → J2000 is `R_x(−ε₀)`, ε₀ = 84381.448″ (IAU 1976, as in SPICE).
- Epochs are TDB seconds past J2000. No UTC↔TDB conversion happens in M1;
  `elements_from_config` therefore takes `epoch_tdb_s` explicitly.

## F. Known limitations and next milestone

### Deviations from the plan, and the choices behind them

1. **GM☉ value.** The approved plan text said "GM_sun = 1.32712440018e20". The milestone brief
   asks for the DE440 value consistent with `gm_de440.tpc` (used in M2), so M1 uses
   1.3271244004127942e20, verified in the kernel. The older value differs by 1.7e-10 relative,
   and a test rejects it.
2. **The state classes live in `frames.py`.** `CartesianState` (the brief's
   "SatelliteState/CartesianState") is defined in `frames.py`, not `orbit.py`. The frame
   transforms need it, and `orbit.py` imports it. That avoids a circular import and lets
   M2/M3 use it without importing the orbit model. A richer `SatelliteState`, with mass,
   attitude and force-model flags (PDF p.4), is not created yet: it would be an unused stub
   until M2 adds the force model.
3. **Propagation returns a structured result.** `propagate_kepler` returns a
   `KeplerPropagation` (a trajectory with (N, 3) position and velocity arrays, plus the
   elements and solver diagnostics) instead of bare arrays, following the "no unstructured
   data" rule.
4. **`require_same_frame_and_origin`** is a small guard added for the PDF §3 frame rule. It is
   tested now and will be used by M2/M3.
5. **Mean sidereal day vs stellar day.** `STELLAR_DAY_S` (named `SIDEREAL_DAY_S` before the
   review) is the stellar day from the IERS
   ERA rate (86 164.0989 s), i.e. the inertial rotation period. It is not the
   equinox-referenced mean sidereal day (86 164.0905 s). The pck00011 IAU_EARTH rate gives a
   period 3.1e-8 shorter. M3's "one rotation per sidereal day" test with IAU_EARTH
   must use that frame's own rate and say so (documented in the constant's docstring).

### Limitations

- **Supported cases.** Only elliptic orbits (e < 1) are supported. Hyperbolic and parabolic
  states raise `ValueError`. Near e → 1 the Kepler solve stays accurate (backward error at
  round-off) but needs up to about 20 iterations at e = 0.999999 and up to ~37 for
  1 − e ≤ 1e-8. For 1 − e < 4e-7 and small M the float64 root itself is only determined to
  ~2√2 ε/√(1 − e) rad, which can exceed 1e-12 rad (irrelevant for heliocentric collectors).
- **Sun-centred only.** Elements and L0 propagation are Sun-centred (`origin="SUN"`).
  Combining with SSB states needs the Sun's SSB state from the ephemeris (M2);
  `state_to_elements` refuses non-Sun origins.
- **Round-off.** The mean anomaly carries absolute round-off ε·|nΔt| (about 1e-13 rad after
  100 revolutions). This matters only for multi-century spans.
- **No SPICE cross-check yet.** The obliquity rotation is not yet cross-checked against SPICE
  `pxform('ECLIPJ2000', 'J2000')`, and GM☉ is not yet compared with the value SPICE loads.
  Both are M2 tasks.
- **Reviewed.** The adversarial review findings and their dispositions are in §G.

### Next milestone

M2, ephemeris geometry (L1/L2):

- kernel fetch with SHA-256 checks (naif0012, pck00011, gm_de440, de440s);
- UTC→TDB via `str2et`;
- an `EphemerisProvider` (SPICE plus an analytic test implementation) that gives J2000/SSB
  states, in SI;
- radial SRP;
- the L1 `solve_ivp` DOP853 propagator in the barycentric frame;
- cross-checks: L1 with planets off equals L0, Earth–Sun range vs JPL, REBOUND, the SPICE
  `pxform` check of `ecliptic_j2000_to_j2000_matrix`, and GM☉ consistency with `gm_de440.tpc`.

## G. Review outcomes

All 11 findings were checked by hand before any change. Each one was confirmed, and each was
fixed. Findings 3 and 7 describe the same defect, so one fix covers both. Nothing was refuted
or deferred.

1. **`solve_kepler` 2-cycle for 1e-10 ≤ 1 − e ≤ 1e-8, small M: fixed.**
   - *Reproduced:* `solve_kepler(1.5301179982083837e-12, 0.99999999, max_iter=1000)` raised.
   - *Fix:* the solver now also stops when the residual is at round-off,
     |f| ≤ 4 ε max(x, E). The constant is `KEPLER_RESIDUAL_ULP_FACTOR`, and that iteration's
     Newton step is still applied. The docstrings and `docs/model_assumptions.md` (Kepler
     step 4) now state the float64 guarantee exactly: the forward error is
     ≲ 2√2 ε/√(1 − e), which is below 1e-12 rad when 1 − e > 4e-7. They also say that
     `tol_rad` is absolute.
   - *Related fix found while checking:* the range reduction mod(M + π, 2π) − π added up to
     ε·π of absolute error to small |M|. With f′ ~ 1e-8 that turned into a forward error of
     2e-8 rad (M = 1e-14). M already in [−π, π) is now used unchanged.
   - *Result:* the reviewer's scan (200 000 log-spaced M in [1e-320, 3.1], with
     1 − e ∈ {3e-8, 1e-8, 3e-9, 1e-9, 1e-10, 1e-12}) converges everywhere in 25–37
     iterations. Results for ordinary e did not change: same iteration counts and residual
