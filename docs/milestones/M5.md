## A. Summary

- **Aperture fields** (`aperture.py`). `ApertureField` gives the radial field `A(r)` for
  `uniform_circular`, `truncated_gaussian` (w0 ≤ D/2) and `parabolic_on_pedestal` (edge
  intensity taper T dB). Each field is normalised in closed form to 1 W leaving the aperture.
  - **Spill-over has one owner.** The truncated Gaussian is renormalised inside the aperture.
    The clipped source power `exp(−2a²/w0²)` belongs to `transmitter.optics_or_rf_efficiency`,
    as M0 defined; M5 only reports it, as `source_spillover_fraction`.
  - The taper efficiency is a diagnostic only and is never applied as an extra factor.
- **Propagation core** (`aperture.py`). It is shared by the optical and microwave models:
  - the scalar Fresnel–Hankel field `U(ρ; z)` by adaptive composite Gauss–Legendre quadrature,
    with at least 16 nodes per oscillation cycle;
  - the Fraunhofer radiant intensity `J(θ)`, in closed form for uniform (Airy) and
    parabolic-on-pedestal (Lommel), by quadrature for the truncated Gaussian;
  - the Fresnel number `N_F` for every epoch;
  - `BeamModelValidityError` for a far-field model with `N_F > models.fraunhofer_max_fresnel_number`;
  - a paraxial check of the Fresnel propagator (neglected quartic phase ≤ 0.1 rad, an assumed
    threshold after Goodman eq. 4-18), applied at construction and on every field evaluation;
  - the single owner of the wavefront (Ruze) loss.
- **Optical models** (`optical.py`).
  - `AiryPattern`: closed-form intensity, encircled energy (EE), first null `1.21967 λR/D`,
    Fresnel-number check.
  - `GaussianBeamPattern`: an untruncated TEM00 beam, `w(z)`, closed-form EE. It is a separate
    model; its comparison path is `fresnel_hankel` with the same truncated field.
- **Capture** (`capture.py`).
  - The exact 1-D arc-length integral `∫I(ρ)·2φ(ρ;d,a)·ρ dρ` for an offset disk.
  - A 2-D Cartesian cross-check, an elliptical receiver (2-D grid) and an exact polar form for a
    centred ellipse.
  - The receiver projection (`horizontal`: equal-area circle `a√(sin el)`; `tracking`: `a`).
  - Pointing: bias plus isotropic Gaussian jitter gives a Rice-distributed offset. The
    expectation uses deterministic Gauss–Legendre quadrature over the Rice density (its
    Gaussian limit for ν/s ≥ 1e4). Percentiles p05/p50/p95 are exact when η(d) is monotone,
    otherwise from the cubic-spline level set (`level_set`, ≤ 1e-8; review M5).
- **Microwave** (`microwave.py`).
  - The filled tapered aperture uses the same machinery.
  - The sparse square lattice has an array factor (peak N, grating lobes at mλ/d), an element
    pattern `cos^q`, and a large-array main-lobe fraction `1/Σ cos^{q−1}θ_mn`, checked against
    hemisphere integration.
  - No coherent combining: `BeamResult` refuses `coherent_combining=True`.
- **Atmosphere** (`atmosphere.py`). Terminal segment only; it depends on elevation and never on
  range.
  - `none`.
  - `scenario`: plane-parallel air mass; optical `τ_z^X`; microwave dB × X; a cloudy optical
    scenario with τ = 0 is a hard outage. A Kasten–Young comparison function is provided.
  - New `itu_r` option for microwave: the `itur` 0.4.0 wrapper (P.618-13 / P.676-12 / P.840-7,
    gas + cloud + rain), with versions pinned and recorded, the configured polarisation tilt
    recorded, itur warnings captured, and calls serialised by a lock. With P.676-11 / P.1511-1
    pinned it reproduces two ITU-R SG3 validation examples to 1e-5 dB. The production P.676-12
    gas term is regression-pinned only, not validated.
- **`BeamResult`** (`beam.py`). It covers the PDF p.4 fields and more, vectorised over epochs:
  - wavelength, aperture, range, elevation, `beam_model`, `fresnel_number` and its limit;
  - `capture_fraction` (expected), `capture_fraction_nominal` (perfect pointing), `_bias_only`
    and `_p05/_p50/_p95`;
  - `pointing_loss = expected/nominal`, `wavefront_efficiency`, `lattice_main_lobe_fraction`,
    the projected receiver radius;
  - peak irradiance per W, plus W m⁻² and the `max_power_density` flag when the power is given;
  - diagnostics, including the per-epoch receiver-projection error; validity notes;
    `scalar_diffraction=True`; `coherent_combining=False`.

  It is computed directly per epoch, with no interpolation table: 3700 MVP epochs take about
  4 s.
- **MVP year** (SPICE, 3700 visible epochs; the satellite is 1.12–1.18 au from the receiver).
  - Far field holds all year: N_F = 5.7–6.0e-4.
  - The Airy first null is at 20.5–21.5 km. The projected receiver radius is 417–997 m.
  - Nominal capture is 0.148–0.834 % (median 0.631 %).
  - The pointing loss with σ = 1 nrad is 4.9e-4.
  - Clear-sky atmosphere factor: 1.
  - This is a negative result: under the PDF example design a 10 m / 1 µm aperture delivers
    under 1 % of the beam to a 1 km receiver. It is recorded, not tuned away.

## E. Assumptions and equations implemented

The full text is in `docs/model_assumptions.md`, "M5"; data are in `docs/data_provenance.md`.
In brief:

- **Aperture normalisation and taper.**
  - Normalisation: `∫|A|²dA = 1`.
  - Gaussian: `c² = 1/[(πw0²/2)(1 − e^{−2a²/w0²})]`.
  - Pedestal: `c² = 1/[πa²(C + (1−C)²/3)]`, `C = 10^{−T/20}`.
  - Taper efficiency: `|∫A dA|²/(πa²)` (Balanis §15.4).
- **Fresnel–Hankel** (Goodman 2005, eq. 4-17): `U(ρ;z) = (2π/(λz))e^{iπρ²/(λz)}∫A e^{iπr²/(λz)}J0(2πrρ/(λz))r dr`.
  Parseval conserves power. The paraxial check bounds the neglected quartic phase of Goodman
  eq. 4-18 by an assumed 0.1 rad (`FRESNEL_PARAXIAL_MAX_PHASE_RAD`), at every evaluated radius.
- **Fraunhofer.**
  - `J(θ) = (1/λ²)|2π∫A J0(2πrθ/λ) r dr|²`.
  - Airy: `I0[2J1(x)/x]²`, `I0 = πD²/(4λ²R²)`, `EE = 1 − J0² − J1²` (Rayleigh; Born & Wolf §8.5.2).
  - Parabolic on pedestal: Lommel `2J2(u)/u²`.
- **Validity.** `N_F = D²/(λR) ≤ 0.5` for far-field models (IEEE Std 145-2013; Balanis ch. 2).
- **Gaussian beam** (Siegman 1986, ch. 17): `w(z) = w0√(1 + (z/z_R)²)`, `EE = 1 − e^{−2ρ²/w²}`.
- **Capture.** `η = ∫I·2φρ dρ`, `φ = arccos((ρ² + d² − a²)/(2ρd))`. For a horizontal receiver
  `a_eff = a√(sin el)`; the error table is in `model_assumptions.md`, and the error is reported
  per epoch.
- **Pointing.**
  - Offset: `d ~ Rice(Rb, Rσ)`.
  - Expectation: `E[η] = ∫η f_Rice`.
  - Monotonicity: Anderson (1955), Proc. AMS 6, 170, for unimodal beams.
- **Wavefront.** `exp(−(2πσ_w/λ)²)` (Ruze 1966, Proc. IEEE 54, 633). The scattered halo is
  assumed not captured.
- **Lattice.** `AF = D_n(ψx)D_n(ψy)` (Balanis eq. 6-89); `η_lattice = 1/Σcos^{q−1}θ_mn`.
- **Atmosphere.**
  - Plane-parallel: `X = 1/sin el`. Kasten & Young (1989), Appl. Opt. 28, 4735, is the
    comparison.
  - `itu_r`: ITU-R P.618-13 combination without scintillation, `A = A_g + A_c + A_r`, with the
    gas, cloud and auxiliary recommendations as pinned.

## F. Known limitations and next milestone

### Deviations from the plan / task text, and why

1. **Two extra modules, `capture.py` and `beam.py`.** The plan's layout lists
   aperture/optical/microwave/atmosphere. Capture, pointing and `BeamResult` are shared by the
   optical and microwave models, so they live in modules of their own rather than in
   `optical.py`, which `microwave.py` would otherwise have to import. The propagation core
   ("shared propagation core") is in `aperture.py`.
2. **Pointing loss is relative to perfect pointing.** The task wrote "nominal (no jitter)";
   here `capture_fraction_nominal` has no bias and no jitter, so `pointing_loss` covers both.
   `capture_fraction_bias_only` is reported separately.
3. **No interpolation table.** Capture is computed directly per epoch (about 1 ms per epoch for
   Airy with jitter), so the "< 1e-6 interpolation error" path was not needed.
4. **The air-mass model stays plane-parallel.** M0 had already fixed it, with its elevation
   floor; Kasten–Young is the named comparison function only.
5. **`array_element_pattern_exponent` defaults to None and is required with a lattice.** A
   defaulted 1.0 would have been indistinguishable from user input, and M0 rejects settings
   that would be ignored.
6. **Monotonicity of E[η] in σ is not universal.** The PDF §13.2 statement holds for the centred
   baseline (a unimodal beam, or a receiver inside the central lobe). For a receiver that cuts
   the first Airy ring, jitter raises capture. This is shown in a test and documented rather
   than hidden.
7. **The `itur` validation values are second-hand.** They are the ITU SG3 validation examples
   as transcribed in itur's test file; the ITU spreadsheet itself was not fetched. itur
   implements P.618-13 and P.676-12, not the newest in-force revisions. The validation
   covers the wrapper with P.676-11 / P.1511-1 pinned; the production P.676-12 / P.1511-2
   path has no validation set and is regression-pinned only (open item: validate against
   P.676-12/-13 and P.618-14).

### Limitations

- **Scalar diffraction only.** There is no turbulence, beam wander, thermal blooming, obscuration
  or polarisation (see "Neglected effects" in `model_assumptions.md`).
- **Equal-area receiver.** It is optimistic by up to 0.2 % in the MVP regime, and by tens of
  percent when the receiver is comparable to the beam at low elevation. This is reported per
  epoch. The exact ellipse is used only for the centred error estimate, not for capture with
  offsets.
- **Pointing quadrature budget.** Jitter more than ~100× the beam feature scale exceeds the
  offset-quadrature budget and raises. Huge near-field receivers raise on the Hankel work
  budget. Both raise clearly; neither is silent.
- **ITU-R level.** The ITU-R attenuation is a deterministic exceedance level, not a time series.
  Scintillation is excluded. The ellipsoidal height is used as height above MSL.
- **Lattice geometry.** The lattice model assumes a broadside, large array; scan loss and
  mutual coupling are not modelled.
