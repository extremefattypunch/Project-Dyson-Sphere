## A. Summary

### What was built

- **One evaluation path for every study** (`evaluation.py`): a design = dotted overrides of a
  base run configuration; a **fidelity level** = stated overrides (+ removed keys) and a capture
  backend; the evaluation runs the validated forward simulator (M6 single-satellite chain or the
  M7 constellation chain with its scheduler) and reads named metrics from the run's own
  summary/time series. Failed designs (invalid configuration, beam model outside its validity)
  are returned as failures with their error, never dropped. `economics.<name>` /
  `mass_proxy.<field>` keys vary economic inputs and assumed specific masses without touching
  the run. Synthetic evaluators (sphere, ZDT1, Binh–Korn, Ishigami, linear, Airy aperture/mass)
  use the same interface so the machinery is verified on problems with known answers.
  Parallel evaluation: spawn process pool, chunked, results in task order (independent of the
  worker count; tested).
- **Screening option** `capture_percentiles=False` (batched kernel only): skips the
  p05/p50/p95 capture diagnostics, whose level-set evaluation cost ~0.13 s per epoch and made a
  jittery design take 2 min instead of 12 s; every power and energy is bit-identical (tested).
- **Optimisation** (`optimization.py`, PDF §12.1–12.3): study files with real/log/integer/
  categorical-bundle variables, objectives (Pareto, never a weighted score), inequality
  constraints incl. a failed-evaluation constraint; scipy `minimize_scalar` sanity check against
  a sweep; pymoo 0.6.2 NSGA-II through ask/tell with its own seeded Generator; per seed every
  evaluated design, the final population, the archive front and HV per generation (Parquet) +
  `run.json` with code/package/Slurm provenance; collation of the union front with seed
  agreement; high-fidelity re-evaluation of selected front designs as complete run directories.
- **Uncertainty** (`uncertainty.py`, PDF §12.4): epistemic and aleatory inputs listed
  separately with provenance; seeded nested Monte Carlo (separate streams) with the
  law-of-total-variance split; SALib Sobol (first/total order, bootstrap CIs, prefix
  convergence); explicit scenarios with a load-time check that the triangular ranges equal the
  scenario values; array-splittable parts with a merge that requires every sample exactly once.
- **CLI**: `helioswarm optimize CONFIG --out DIR [--seed S | --array-index I] [--workers N]
  [--collate | --reevaluate | --sanity]` (PDF-required command; without options: every seed +
  collation), `helioswarm uncertainty CONFIG --out DIR [--part ...] [--array-index/--array-count]
  [--collate]`; `validate-config` accepts the study files.
- **Closed deferrals**: §8.4 PV technology scenarios with **sourced** cell data (AZUR SPACE
  3G30A-Advanced datasheet; JPL Pub. 78-15 Vol. VIII silicon K7 cells; both fetched, checksummed
  and their records checked at the publisher/NTRS; derivations tested); §14.3.3 coarse-to-fine
  (screening search → high-fidelity re-evaluation; **partial** after the review: far-field
  designs only, F); §6.2 L3 as an SRP area-to-mass sensitivity
  case (radial SRP is exact in direction for the sun-tracking collector).
- **Moved items from M8a**: (i) helio-vs-GEO economics on one economic boundary (GEO
  optimistic/conservative cost files added; the helio/GEO files differ only in the transport Δv
  model, tested), (ii) the M7b comparison re-run with ERA5 weather (ten years) at the
  comparison site and at the ERA5-chosen clear site aswan.

### Results (Slurm runs; one simulated year 2030; every design value assumed unless stated)

**Sanity check** (`configs/optimization.yaml`, aperture with a 20 W/m² aperture penalty,
screening fidelity, other variables nominal): scipy bounded Brent `D* = 70.33 m`
(J = 134 438 W, 11 evaluations) vs the best of a 21-point sweep 70.74 m (J = 134 428 W): agree.
The physics-derived closed form (Airy EE minus areal mass, `J1(x)/x = √(cπ/4P0)/β`) is
recovered by scipy to 1e-5 and by NSGA-II to 1 % (tests).

(`figures/m8/optimization_helio_pareto.png`, `…_pareto_union.csv`). Seed agreement **not met**
(spread 7.7 % > 5 %; three seeds within 1 %, F). A 30-generation pilot (two seeds, job
60475688) met the per-seed rule but disagreed by 11.7 % between seeds, which is why the study
was rerun with 100 generations. Patterns on the front (all bounds and limits assumed): the
wavelength sits at its lower bound (0.80–0.89 µm for 75 % of the front: smaller λ narrows the
beam), the receiver radius near its upper bound (median 9.8 km), the minimum elevation at its
10° lower bound, the pointing jitter below the 5 nrad constraint (the receiver radius trend is
an artefact of the formulation: receiver infrastructure is not penalised by any objective or
constraint, F), and the **receiver-capacity
constraint (10 MW) binds** for 55 front designs; thermal feasibility holds on the whole front
(radiator temperature limits mostly 517–540 K are chosen). The **orbit variables exploit the one-year objective window**: 290
of 487 designs have a < 0.8 au (mostly 0.70–0.74 au) with phases that bring the satellite to
0.27–0.34 au of the Earth during 2030 (re-evaluated designs c00, c03–c05), the best a ≈ 1 au design delivers 1.19 MW against 1.67 MW for the best
drifting one — a window artefact, reported as a negative result (F).

**Technology study** (categorical optical vs microwave; `configs/optimization_technology.yaml`)
(24 × 15 × 2 seeds, 690 evaluations, jobs 60477024 seed 1, 60479896 seed 2): **microwave is
eliminated**: of 85 microwave evaluations the best delivered 9 W (2.3 km array, 205 000 t),
against up to 2.76 MW for optical designs; the union front (90 designs) is entirely optical.
The 10 failed designs are optical apertures of 0.4–1.3 km outside the Airy far-field validity
(recorded, infeasible). The two seeds disagree strongly (final HV 2.2e14 vs 1.1e15, spread
80 %): seed 1 never explored collector areas above 2 × 10⁴ m² (front ends at 449 kW), seed 2
reached the 10⁵ m² bound (2.76 MW at 133 t) — 24 × 15 is too small a search for a converged
front (per-seed rule not met for either seed); the categorical conclusion does not depend on
it: the lightest optical front design (1.8 t) already delivers 0.46 kW, 50 times the best
microwave design at 10⁵ times its mass.
Re-evaluation of four front designs at `high` (L1, 974 s, reference capture; clear sky for
the optical choice as at screening; job 60486043, snapshot `…132446Z`): P_grid within
2.2e-4 and mass within 4e-5 of the screening values, all feasible.

**Constellation study** (`configs/optimization_constellation.yaml`; 16 × 10 × 2 seeds, 287
network-year evaluations, job 60477744): seed HV spread 2.6 % (agree) but neither seed met the
per-seed rule after 10 generations (exploratory). Union front 31 designs from 4.3 kW / 13 t (2

The screening model is validated for these designs: L0 → L1 (planets, SRP), 3896 s → 974 s
and the batched → reference capture change P_grid by ≤ 0.18 %, availability by ≤ 0.3 %, peak
captured power and irradiance by ≤ 0.1 %, and every design stays feasible (`high_clear`).
**Weather is not**: with ERA5 cloud outages at the clear-sky-selected example site, every
selected design loses 57–61 % of its energy and falls below the 0.2 availability constraint —
none of the six is feasible at high fidelity. This is the coarse-to-fine finding the PDF asks
for (§17): a clear-sky screening search at a cloudy site selects designs that real weather
makes infeasible; the screening level must include weather (or the site must be chosen from
ERA5) before its fronts are design guidance. The close-approach designs also deliver part of
their energy near conjunction (energy-weighted 5th-percentile Sun–satellite separation seen from
the receiver 6–22° for c00/c02–c05; 15–23 % of c00/c03–c05's energy at ranges < 0.4 au; the eccentric c02 stays within 0.19–0.39 au
all year),
which a Sun-avoidance cone would remove (F).

Constellation re-evaluation (3 designs, L1 stacked, reference capture, ERA5 at the three demo
sites; job 60481984): network P_grid −22 % to −27 %, network availability 1.0 → 0.80–0.93; the
largest design (two rings, 21 satellites) stays feasible, the two 2-satellite designs violate
the 0.9 availability requirement.

**GPU** — **not used in production; benchmark not run** (stated plainly). Profiling of screening
evaluations on the CPU (`scripts/bench_m8_evaluation.py`, six designs of the main study, in
session, saved as `$HELIOSWARM_RUNS_DIR/gpu_bench_m8/cpu_only_session.json`; a first run gave
the same split within 0.1 s): 2.58–3.55 s per one-year evaluation, of which the batched Airy
capture is 36–51 % (0.92–1.82 s; orbit 0.05 s, illumination 0.46 s, solar/thermal 0.15 s,
access 1.0 s; percentiles off). By Amdahl's law a GPU capture kernel could at most speed one evaluation up 1.6–2.0×,
whereas the population is embarrassingly parallel over CPU cores (8 workers per job, 4 seeds
in parallel). A per-design JAX call would also recompile for every new array shape (the number of access
epochs differs between designs).
PDF §14.3.5 asks for GPU code only where profiling shows a clear bottleneck: capture does not
dominate here, so the studies ran on CPU workers. The GPU job (`slurm/m8_gpu_bench.sbatch`,
CPU-vs-JAX per design) is ready but was **not submitted**: both `gpu_test` slots of the account
were held by the user's CryoSPARC jobs (60465092, 60465098, ~8 h of their time limit left) for
the whole session, and paying for `gpu`/`gpu_requeue` was not justified by the profile. The M7
measurement of the JAX kernel on a real GPU (`docs/milestones/M7.md` D) remains the GPU evidence.

## E. Assumptions and equations implemented (with references)

Full statement in `docs/model_assumptions.md` (M8); provenance in `docs/data_provenance.md` (M8).

- **Design evaluation**: base configuration → fidelity overrides → design overrides → validated
  `RunConfig` → forward simulator of record. Metric `availability_above_threshold =
  Σ w_k [P_grid,k ≥ P_min] / Σ w_k` (trapezoid weights, as `availability`); mass proxy
  `m_bus + s_col A_col + s_rad A_rad + s_tx πD²/4` (M7, assumed specific masses); LCOE from
  `economics.evaluate_economics` (M8a; assumed economics).
- **Encoding**: `x = lo + u (hi − lo)`; log: `exp(ln lo + u ln(hi/lo))`; integer
  `min(⌊lo + u (hi − lo + 1)⌋, hi)`; categorical `choices[min(⌊u n⌋, n − 1)]` (bundles).
- **NSGA-II** (Deb et al. 2002, IEEE TEC 6(2) 182; pymoo 0.6.2: SBX η = 15, p = 0.9;
  polynomial mutation η = 20; constraint domination); objectives `F = metric` or `−metric`;
  constraints `g = (v − limit)/s` (≤) or `(limit − v)/s` (≥) plus `g_evaluation`.
- **Hypervolume** (Zitzler & Thiele 1999) of the feasible non-dominated archive against the
  configured reference point; per-seed convergence `|HV_G − HV_{G−w}|/HV_G ≤ tol`; seed
  agreement `(max − min)/max ≤ tol`.
- **Closed-form physics optimum** (test): `J(D) = P0 EE(βD) − c πD²/4`, `EE(x) = 1 − J0² − J1²`
  (Born & Wolf §8.5.2), `dEE/dx = 2J1(x)²/x` → `J1(x*)/x* = √(cπ/(4P0))/β`, `D* = x*/β`.
- **Nested MC**: `SeedSequence(seed).spawn(2)`; `x = F⁻¹(u)`; law of total variance
  `Var Y = E_i[Var_j Y] + Var_i[E_j Y]`.
- **Sobol** (Sobol' 2001, Math. Comput. Simul. 55, 271; Saltelli et al. 2010, Comput. Phys.
  Commun. 181, 259; Jansen 1999): SALib 1.6, `calc_second_order=False`, bootstrap CIs.
  Ishigami test values (Ishigami & Homma 1990): `S1 = (0.3139, 0.4424, 0)`,
  `ST = (0.5576, 0.4424, 0.2437)` from `V1 = ½(1 + bπ⁴/5)²`, `V2 = a²/8`,
  `V13 = b²π⁸(1/18 − 1/50)`.
- **PV technology**: `η_ref = η_incident/α`; `γ = (dVmp/dT)/Vmp + (dImp/dT)/Imp`;
  `η_ref(300 K) = η_ref(T_s)(1 + γ(300 − T_s))` (M4 linear law).
- **L3 SRP**: sun-tracking flat plate → radial force ∝ A_col; sensitivity in A/m.

## F. Known limitations and next milestone

- **One-year objective window (negative result for orbit optimisation).** `p_grid_mean_W` is a
  mean over the simulated year 2030. For orbits that drift relative to the Earth (a ≠ 1 au, or
  e > 0) one year samples only part of the synodic cycle (1.76 yr at 0.74 au), and NSGA-II
  exploits this: 290 of the 487 union-front designs sit at a < 0.8 au with phases that put the
  close approach (range 0.27–0.34 au) inside 2030; a = 1 au fronts reach 1.19 MW vs
  1.67 MW for these. The orbit variables of the shipped study therefore optimise a window
  artefact, not a lifetime-averaged design; a lifetime- or synodic-period-averaged objective is
  needed before orbit optima are meaningful (M9 or later). The technology, aperture, receiver
  and thermal trends (bounds active at the smallest wavelength, the largest receiver radius,
  the lowest minimum elevation; receiver capacity binding for 55 front designs) do not depend
  on this.
- **Receiver infrastructure unpenalised (M8 review).** PDF §12.2 lists "minimize collector area
  or receiver infrastructure where appropriate"; the shipped studies have no objective or
  constraint on the receiver (the `receiver_area_m2` metric exists but is unused; the mass proxy
  is the satellite's). The main study therefore pushes `receiver.radius_m` to its 10 km bound at
  no cost: 82 % of the 487 union-front designs have R > 9 km (median 9.82 km, ~300 km² of
  receiver). The receiver-radius result is an artefact of the formulation, not a design finding;
  a `receiver_area_m2` objective or a receiver-cost constraint is needed (M9 or later; would
  require re-running the study). Compliance §12.2: partial.
- **Far-field search only; no Fresnel at high fidelity (M8 review).** Both fidelity levels of the
  optical studies use `models.beam = airy_far_field`; designs with N_F > 0.5 raise
  `BeamModelValidityError` at screening (158 failed designs in the main study, 10 optical
  apertures of 0.4–1.3 km in the technology study) and are never evaluated with the
  `fresnel_hankel` model the code has (used by GEO). For the re-evaluated finalists Airy *is*
  the high-fidelity answer — their largest N_F is 0.21 (c00; ≤ 0.0055 for c01–c05) and at that
  epoch the M5 Fresnel–Hankel pattern gives the same centred capture within 1.8e-7 and a 0.23 %
  lower peak irradiance (`scripts/m8_fresnel_vs_airy_check.py`, in session on the saved `high`
  runs) — but the PDF §14.3.3 "high-fidelity propagation for finalists" is not available for
  near-field designs, which the search excludes. Compliance §14.3.3: partial.
- **Idealised settings exploited by the optimiser** (as labelled in every run report): no Sun
  avoidance cone at the receiver (`receiver.sun_avoidance_deg = 0`; conjunction geometry looks
  towards the Sun), clear sky at screening, no laser-PV flux or eye-safety limit other than the
  study's assumed 1000 W/m² cap. The re-evaluation shows that clear-sky screening at the
  cloudy example site selects designs that ERA5 weather makes infeasible (A); weather or an
  ERA5-chosen site must enter the search level before its fronts are design guidance.
- **Seed agreement not reached** for the main study: final hypervolumes 1.436 / 1.441 / 1.338 /
  1.450 × 10¹² (spread 7.7 % > 5 %); three seeds agree within 1 %, seed 20261116 stalled from
  generation ~60. The 30-generation pilot (two seeds) disagreed by 11.7 %. The union front of all
  seeds is reported; the stalled seed shows that 4 000 evaluations of a 13-variable problem do
  not guarantee a converged front.
- **Screening is weather-blind** (clear sky): receiver sites are not optimised; site choice uses
  ERA5 directly (M8a climatology, the aswan comparison here).
- **Pointing bound limited by cost**: jitter up to 10 nrad only; with σD/λ ≫ 1 the offset
  quadrature of the expected capture costs minutes per design (measured: 1e-7 rad, D = 100 m,
  > 10 min) for designs that are poor anyway.
- **Dropped for cost** (no silent caps): Sobol indices for the GEO baseline (~3 300 evaluations
  at ~150 s) and for the constellation (≥ 1 152 network-years); both have nested Monte Carlo and
  (GEO) scenarios. The technology and constellation studies use small populations (24 × 15,
  16 × 10) and are exploratory; their fronts are not converged (per-seed rule not met).
- **Uncertainty model**: operational randomness (random outages, scheduling realisations) is not
  sampled; pointing jitter is an epistemic sigma whose realisations are integrated analytically
  (expected capture), only the pointing bias is a sampled aleatory realisation; inputs independent; equal weather-year probabilities; the Rayleigh
  pointing bias and uniform wavefront error are assumed; the economics MC of M8a is not
  repeated here (only launch and manufacturing costs vary with the physics); LCOE for single
  satellites uses the economics module's specific masses, so the radiator specific mass enters
  the mass proxy but not the LCOE (Sobol index 0, as it should under that boundary).
- **SRP L3**: only the A/m sensitivity of the radial model; non-radial SRP for a fixed-normal
  attitude is not modelled; drift grows beyond the one-year window.
- **PV data**: the III-V values are BOL datasheet values (radiation degradation enters only
  through the economics' annual degradation); the silicon case is a 1979 cell (legacy, labelled
  conservative); absorptivity of the K7 cell is assumed (0.90).
